# Why some responders stopped getting work after 4.2: working hypothesis

*Draft for Marcus, Wen, Ravi, Nadia, Sofia and Helen · 7 Oct 2026*

## The hypothesis in one line

**Based on what I found, the reason some responders are getting no pings at all is that a few missed pings after the wait was cut to 60 seconds dropped their ranking below a nearby rival's, and they can't climb back, because the system counts a miss the same as a "no", never lets a score recover on its own, and only asks the next person if the one above says no, so once you're behind you're never asked, and you can't earn your ranking back without being asked.**

The proximity reweighting in 4.2 is **not** the cause. It actually softened the effect (see below), so this is not an argument for reverting it.

## How the loop works

1. **A shorter wait means more misses.** Missed pings went from 2% to 18% of all pings when the wait dropped from 90s to 60s.
2. **A miss costs as much as a "no".** The routing code treats a missed ping exactly like a turn-down (−0.12), and a take only earns +0.08.
3. **The score never recovers on its own.** It only goes back up when the responder takes a job. There's also one score per person, not one per area, so misses far from home count against you at home.
4. **A lower score means you're asked later.** Responders are pinged one at a time and the process stops at the first yes. If you're second in line, you almost never hear about the job.
5. **Not being pinged means you can't recover.** Meanwhile, every job your rival takes raises their score. The gap widens instead of closing.

This matches the support tickets: 30 of the 45 callout tickets since release say "my phone never goes off", and 15 say "it was gone before I could answer".

## Evidence

**All four affected responders followed the same timeline.**

| Responder | Area | Before 4.2 | Week of 10 Aug | From late Aug |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vesper | Old Town | ~14 pings/wk, 0–1 missed | 4 missed | 1–2 pings/wk, none taken |
| Meteor Mite | Eastgate | ~11/wk, 0–1 missed | 4 missed | 1–2/wk, none taken |
| The Undertow | Harborside | ~12/wk, 0–1 missed | 5 missed | ~1/wk, none taken |
| Farlight | Uptown | ~12/wk, 0–1 missed | 4 missed | ~1/wk, then none |

Misses spiked the week 4.2 shipped, and pings collapsed the following week. That's the order the loop predicts.

**The work went to other people, not away.** Harborside and Uptown were back to about 10 callouts a week by late August. Nightwell, The Gale, Stormwrack and Captain Vantage each got 20–35% more pings.

**Replaying the scores shows the mechanism.** These figures apply the repo's scoring rules to the ping history:
- **Meteor Mite vs The Gale (both Eastgate):** Mite led 0.76 to 0.72 on 13 Aug. Mite missed two pings, and The Gale took both jobs. By 17 Aug the scores were 0.56 vs 0.84–1.0, and The Gale was pinged first on about 25 Eastgate callouts in a row. Two misses decided who got a whole area's work for a month.
- **The Undertow:** his score fell from 0.80 to 0.20 in four days, mostly from misses on callouts **outside** Harborside. Responders from Southport, Riverside and Greenway then took nearly all of Harborside's work.
- **Farlight:** fell to about 0.40 within days. Responders from three neighbouring areas, all scoring around 0.9, went first on Uptown callouts.

**Why the weight change isn't the cause.** Under the old weights, a responder with a zero track record lost to anyone with a perfect one within about **40 minutes** of travel. Under the 4.2 weights, that drops to about **19 minutes**. The reweighting helped local responders. It's the jump in misses that pushed people's scores down.

**Seasonality doesn't explain it.** Total callouts dipped from about 145 to 120 a week, but that doesn't explain why the work moved from some responders to others in the same area. Mite (0.8 taken a week) and The Gale (12.9) both cover Eastgate.

## What it doesn't explain yet

- **Vesper on 18 Aug.** She wasn't pinged on an Old Town callout even though her score (0.76) was nearly three times the winner's (Nightwell, 0.28). Either Nightwell was much closer at the time, Vesper wasn't marked available, or production scores differently from the repo.
- **Out-of-area responders on "Old Town" lists.** The code only considers responders in the callout's region, but Midtown, Lakeshore and Hillcrest responders all appear on Old Town callouts. So "region" in the code is wider than handlers' areas, or production differs.
- **Callouts that end after one ping.** About 40 unfilled callouts before 4.2 and 40 after stopped after a single ping, with nobody else asked. This didn't change with 4.2, so it isn't the cause, but it doesn't match the code.

## Options to discuss once confirmed (not decisions)

- **Short term:** reset the four responders' scores so they're back in line.
- **Stop misses counting like turn-downs**, or count them less.
- **Let scores drift back toward the middle over time.** This has been an open question in the code since 2019.
- **Keep a score per area** so far-away misses don't hurt you at home.
- **Revisit the 60s wait**, separately from the proximity change, which this analysis doesn't implicate.

## Caveats

- The scores here are a replay of the repo's rules against the `pings` table, not Dispatch's stored values.
- The data has no location or availability records, so travel time is inferred, not measured.
- Ravi's official weekly numbers haven't been reconciled with this yet.
