Release 4.2: two missed pings, and a responder vanished

Six modules of digging, one prompt at a time. What went wrong, how we know, what we'd build, and how we stop it happening again.

What happened, plainly: 4.2 cut the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds. More pings were missed. The routing code counts a miss the same as a "no" and never lets a score recover on its own. A few misses pushed four responders behind a nearby rival, and once you're behind you stop being pinged, so you can't earn your place back. Their work went to other people.

Why it happened

A loop you can't get out of

  1. 60s wait → more misses. Missed pings went from 2% to 18% of pings after 12 Aug.
  2. A miss costs as much as a "no". −0.12 either way. A take earns only +0.08.
  3. The score never recovers on its own. No decay and no reset. It's one score per person, so far-away misses hurt at home.
  4. Lower score → asked later. Pings go out one at a time and stop at the first yes.
  5. Not pinged → can't take → can't recover. Meanwhile the rival keeps taking jobs and climbing.

Scoring rules from 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/history.py. Still to confirm with Wen: that production scores the same way as the repo.

Keeping it from happening again

Each check targets a gap that let 4.2 slip through

1 · Names who owns it
Every decision has one person to ask.
In 4.2: Marcus's 14 Aug question about who the reweighting applies to went unanswered for weeks. Nobody owned the answer.
2 · Says how we'll know it worked
A target and a time frame, so a drop gets noticed early.
In 4.2: misses ran at 13–21% for four weeks. Meanwhile the drop was put down to the usual August dip.
3 · Scope at the end matches the start
No change quietly rides along with another.
In 4.2: the 60s cut and the reweighting shipped together. How they interacted with scoring was never considered.
4 · Problem before fix
Evidence first, solution second.
In 4.2: the fix for one group (wide-area responders) shipped without asking who else it would affect.
